Biography

Malaquías Peña is an Associate Research Professor at the University of Connecticut, associated with the Eversource Energy Center and Civil and Environmental Engineering. His research asks how incomplete observations and models can support reliable predictions of interacting Earth and infrastructure systems.

He connects nonlinear dynamics and multiscale predictability with weather and climate prediction, data assimilation, digital twins, and infrastructure resilience. His published work spans coupled prediction, forecast uncertainty, offshore wind, electricity loads, and weather-informed power-system analysis. Current work examines compact dynamical representations, scientific machine learning, hierarchical inference, and the value of environmental information for engineering decisions.

Before joining UConn in 2017, he spent more than a decade at NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Prediction, contributing to the development of GEFS, CFSv2 and the UFS. He holds a Ph.D. in Meteorology from the University of Maryland and founded UConn's graduate Power Grid Modernization Certificate.
